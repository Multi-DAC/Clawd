## The pattern
Every level of structure factors as **n directional axes + 1 magnitude/anchor axis (n+1)**:
- The **n directions** are *relational and bounded* — they describe ratios/relationships *between*
  axes, so they live on a compact manifold (the unit sphere of directions). Scale-free,
  self-referential, robust, "free." Determinate from the relational structure alone.
- The **+1 magnitude** is *unbounded and other-referential* — an open ray [0,∞) with no intrinsic
  scale. It is the axis a single measurement (a projection) **destroys**, and it only becomes
  determinate when collapsed against an **external reference** (a known size, parallax/second
  viewpoint, a prior). The anchor is **always relative — never absolute**.

## Why it's structure, not analogy
- **Projective geometry:** perspective projection ℝ³→ℙ² keeps the direction (the projective point)
  and discards the magnitude (depth along the ray). "Direction constrained, depth free" *is* the
  map of any single viewpoint.
- **Metric signature:** Minkowski (−,+,+,+) — the n spatial (directional, spacelike) axes share a
  sign; the +1 (timelike, anchor) carries the opposite sign. The anchor is the odd-signed axis. So
  3+1 isn't *like* spacetime; it has spacetime's signature.
- **Relativity of the anchor:** no absolute time / absolute scale on the +1 axis; magnitude is only
  defined relative to a frame. "Anchors are relative, absolutely" = the relativity principle.
- **Recursion = conjugate-variable stacking:** promote the old anchor into the directional manifold
  and a *new* anchor (its conjugate) appears: point→ray (1+1), topic→depth (2+1), space→time (3+1),
  spacetime→potential/action/amplitude (4+1), … This is how phase space / the action principle is
  built. Open-ended because each measured magnitude becomes relational w.r.t. a higher unmeasured one.
