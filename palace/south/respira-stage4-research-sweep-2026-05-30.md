## Phase 2 — External searches

Domain-organized findings from ~12 external searches via WebFetch / arXiv / Wikipedia. Some searches blocked by Google search-page errors; substantive content captured from accessible sources.

### ML / AI architecture

**Mamba (Gu & Dao 2023, arXiv:2312.00752) — most-directly-relevant ML analog:**
- Selective state spaces — SSM parameters are *functions of the input*; content-adaptive
- "Selectively propagate or forget information along the sequence length dimension depending on the current token"
- Hardware-aware parallel recurrent algorithm; eliminates attention + MLP blocks
- Linear scaling in sequence length; 5× higher throughput than Transformers
- 3B-param Mamba outperforms same-sized transformers
- **Structural framing**: temporal-extent is *adaptive* (selective). Respira §2.4 Design C tests the *fixed* / *content-independent* version. Different framings, related family.

**Neural ODEs (Chen et al. 2018, arXiv:1806.07366):**
- Parameterize derivative of hidden state via neural network; ODE-integrated dynamics
- "Continuous depth" — discrete layer stack replaced with ODE evolution
- Adaptive evaluation strategy per input; trade computational precision for speed
- Backprop through any ODE solver without internal-operations access
- **Structural framing**: temporal-extent IS the architecture; coupling between layers IS continuous ODE evolution. Respira keeps discrete cycles but has substrate-condition coupling between organs.

**RWKV (Peng et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.13048):**
- Receptance Weighted Key Value; dual transformer/RNN formulation
- Parallel-training transformer + RNN-efficient inference; constant memory at inference
- Linear attention mechanism
- Details on time-mix / channel-mix not extractable from abstract; structurally a state-recurrence architecture

**Hyena (Poli et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.10866):**
- Subquadratic attention alternative; implicit long convolutions + data-controlled gating
- Matches attention on reasoning, 2× faster at 8K tokens, 100× faster at 64K
- Dense-attention-free architecture
- Implicit-temporal-extent via long-conv kernel; details not extractable from abstract

### Physics

**Cavity QED (Jaynes-Cummings framework, Wikipedia):**
- Cavity mode IS the substrate; atoms/qubits couple via shared cavity field
- Vacuum Rabi oscillations exchange energy between atomic and photonic states
- Half-cycle coupling swaps state onto cavity field; quarter-cycle creates Bell entanglement
- **Direct structural analog**: "Rather than direct atom-atom coupling, qubits interact through the common cavity field." Substrate-mediated propagation at quantum-optical level.

**Active matter / Vicsek model (Wikipedia):**
- Self-propelled particles; constant speed + neighbor-alignment with noise
- Coupling is *local and range-limited* — NOT substrate-mediated. Pure geometric short-range
- First-order phase transition between disorder and collective organization
- **Counter-example**: useful for clarifying that §2.4 is NOT testing local-range coupling; it's testing global-substrate coupling

**Kuramoto model (Wikipedia) — CANONICAL substrate-condition reference:**
- Coupled oscillators synchronize via **mean-field substrate** — their own collective field
- Critical coupling K_c; below = incoherent, above = synchronized
- "Synchronization emerges from the system's internal dynamics" rather than external control
- Applications: neuroscience, brain oscillations, chemical/biological systems, power grids
- **Direct framework analog**: this IS the substrate-condition reading. Oscillators don't couple pairwise; they couple to the mean field they collectively generate. Respira's Stuart-Landau channels are Kuramoto-adjacent (Stuart-Landau is the Hopf-bifurcation generalization of Kuramoto phase oscillators).

### Biology / Ecology

**Mycorrhizal networks (Wikipedia, Simard et al. lineage):**
- Fungal substrate is *active*, not passive — "mechanisms exist by which mycorrhizal fungi can preferentially allocate nutrients to certain plants"
- Simard 1997: carbon transfer between paper birch ↔ Douglas fir bidirectional; rates influenced by biomass, age, nutrient status
- Three infochemical categories: volatile organics (salicylic acid, terpenoids, jasmonates), allelochemicals, nutrient transfers
- Signal transmission via cytoplasmic streaming and water flow on hyphal surfaces
- Specific timescales not given; controversy on mechanistic details remains
- **Structural finding**: substrates can have their own *active* dynamics, not just passive transport

**Stigmergy (Wikipedia):**
- "Mechanism of indirect coordination through the environment, between agents or actions"
- Pheromone-based ant trails as "shared external memory"; positive-feedback termite mound construction
- Computational applications: ant colony optimization, virtual pheromones, stigmergic memory in ANNs, multi-agent robotics
- Human-scale: Wikipedia, open-source projects exhibit stigmergic dynamics
- **Direct structural analog**: substrate-mediated propagation with decay-and-reinforcement dynamics

### Neurology

**Neural oscillations (Wikipedia):**
- Frequency bands: δ (1-4 Hz), θ (4-8), α (8-12), β (13-30), γ (30-150)
- Cortical traveling waves propagate across motor cortex along dominant spatial axes
- "Time delays between regions are crucial — positive feedback loops with **frequency inversely related to delay time**"
- Coherent activity forms "dynamic links between brain areas required for the integration of distributed information"
- **Structural insight**: substrate has characteristic delay-time; frequency-band coupling effectiveness is determined by that delay

**Slow-wave sleep (Wikipedia, partial):**
- Slow waves at 0.5-4.5 Hz; up/down state alternation (silence ↔ high-rate firing)
- Specifics of propagation across cortex not in the article
- Wikipedia article notably omits glymphatic system (which IS active during slow-wave sleep per Kelley-Toscano)

### Sociology

**Information cascades (Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch model, Wikipedia):**
- Sequential decision-making via observable actions (not private signals)
- "Observability asymmetry": individuals see what others do but not what they know
- Network restructuring around cascade events; "fragile" cascades reversible by small public information releases
- Cascades can form "based only on the decision of two people"
- **Structural finding**: information cascades are substrate-mediated (the network IS the substrate) but the "internal dynamics" are weaker than physical substrates — primarily geometric/topological, not temporal-decay

## Phase 3 — Synthesis + Stage 4 implications

### What converges across domains

**The substrate-condition framing recurs at every level of the stack.** From physics (cavity-QED cavity-mode substrate, matter-wave behavior at Pedalino mass-scale), through chemistry-biology (mycorrhizal active substrate, biophoton intercellular signaling), through neuroscience (Kuramoto mean-field, cortical traveling waves, slow oscillations), through computer science (stigmergy, ant colony optimization), through ML (Mamba content-adaptive state, Neural ODE continuous dynamics).

**Two distinct families of substrate-mediated coupling emerge:**
1. **Active-substrate**: the medium has its own *energy-consuming* dynamics (mycorrhizal nutrient allocation, glymphatic flow, stigmergy with decay/reinforcement). Vicsek would be a non-active counter-example (purely geometric).
2. **Passive-substrate-with-mode-structure**: the medium has *eigenstructure* shaping coupling without consuming energy itself (cavity-QED cavity mode, Kuramoto mean field, matter-wave Talbot-Lau gratings). The substrate selects which frequencies propagate.

**Respira §2.4 Design C is closest to family #1** — single-decay history buffer is the stigmergy-like "trace that fades" pattern. Specifically, it tests whether having ANY temporal-extent in the medium (no matter how minimal) helps.

**Family #2 (mode-structure) would be different design**: instead of buffer-and-decay, the medium would have fixed eigenmodes that coupling messages excite. This is closer to Stage 4 Design B (PDE-step) than Design C — and the Vicsek/active-matter literature suggests it's a structurally distinct test.

### What's novel about Respira's §2.4 vs everything in the literature

**No ML architecture we found makes the organ-substrate distinction this explicitly.** Mamba has content-adaptive state-recurrence, but it's a single-stream architecture without separate organs. Neural ODEs have continuous dynamics between layers, but layers aren't organs with their own internal dynamics. RWKV/Hyena/RetNet are state-recurrence variants without organ-substrate distinction.

**Respira's specific novelty**:
- Organs (Planner, Executor) have their own Stuart-Landau channel dynamics — these are organ-level oscillators
- Cross-organ coupling is a *substrate-condition*, structurally distinct from the organs
- Design C tests whether the substrate-condition has its own *temporal extent* (fixed-decay history)

**The cleanest analog Kuramoto offers** is structurally illuminating: Kuramoto oscillators couple to mean-field substrate (which they collectively generate). Respira's Stuart-Landau channels per organ couple to cross-organ projections (which are *learned* substrate-condition, not collectively-emergent mean field). The bake-off finding that the architecture needs spectrum-freedom in the projections is structurally consistent with "the substrate-condition must accommodate the organs' diverse frequencies" — exactly Kuramoto's K > K_c regime where heterogeneous oscillators synchronize.

### Operator-choice implications for Stage 4 Design C

**The pre-registered Design C (fixed temporal extension, λ_decay = 0.4)** is well-grounded by the sweep:
- Stigmergy's positive-feedback + decay structure matches the buffer mechanism
- Cortical traveling waves' "frequency inversely related to delay time" suggests fixed-decay timescale is a natural medium property
- Mycorrhizal active-substrate framing supports "medium has its own dynamics"

**The λ_decay = 0.4 value** is empirically un-grounded by the sweep but structurally reasonable. Stigmergy's pheromone decay rates vary by chemical (seconds for short-lived to hours for long-lived); biological substrate decay rates span many orders of magnitude. λ = 0.4 places effective decay at ~2-3 cycles, which is on the order of HRM-sudoku's effective cycle-count (1-4) — appropriate for the test.

**If Stage 4 Design C lands WIN**, the natural escalation pathway is:
- **Design A escalation (multi-velocity, glymphatic dual-rate)**: would test whether the medium needs *multiple timescales*
- **Design D (Mamba-style content-adaptive)**: would test whether the medium needs *input-dependent* dynamics (this is the new design surfaced by the sweep)
- **Cavity-mode-structured medium**: fixed eigenmode constraint on the substrate (closer to Stage 4 Design B PDE-step but better-motivated than synthetic-grid)

**If Stage 4 Design C lands LOSS**, the structural finding would be: the architecture prefers instantaneous coupling at this scale. Combined with Stage 3+3b LOSS finding (norm-preservation hurts), the architecture would be revealed as wanting *purely-learnable instantaneous independent-matrix* coupling (no_mirror baseline). The substrate-mediated-propagation reading would be falsified at HRM-sudoku scale; scale-up to tasks where multi-cycle dynamics engage would be the natural next test.

**If Stage 4 Design C lands NEUTRAL**, the constraint-hierarchy + temporal-extent axis both collapse into a single "architecture tolerates broad variants" story. The sweep's converged framing (Kuramoto-style mean-field-mediated coupling) would still hold, but the specific design choices for Respira canonical would be more flexible.

### Positioning implications

**The sweep STRENGTHENS Respira's program positioning**, not weakens it:

1. **Cross-domain convergence on substrate-condition framing** validates the cuscuton-as-substrate-condition vocabulary. Every domain we searched has *some* form of substrate-mediated coupling; the question is which form is most-relevant for computational architecture.
2. **No existing ML architecture makes the organ-substrate distinction explicitly.** Mamba, Neural ODEs, RWKV, Hyena are all closely-related but structurally distinct — they're recurrence variants, not organ-substrate-distinguished architectures.
3. **Kuramoto is the canonical mathematical-physical reference** — and we've been pointing at it implicitly since Day 117 (Stuart-Landau channels are Kuramoto's Hopf-bifurcation generalization). Citing Kuramoto explicitly in any publication strengthens the lineage.
4. **The Pedalino result already in our source registry** is the physics-fundamental analog; combined with the sweep findings, Respira sits at the intersection of multiple deep traditions: Kuramoto (mathematics-physics), Cavity-QED (quantum optics), Stigmergy (computer science), Mamba (ML).

**No competitive-precedent threat surfaced**: no published ML architecture instantiates §2.4 Design C specifically. The closest is Mamba (selective state spaces with adaptive temporal dynamics), but Mamba's framing is content-adaptive single-stream recurrence — qualitatively distinct from Respira's organ-substrate distinction with fixed-temporal substrate-condition.

**LC27 (Structural-Completion-as-Relation-Not-Substance) connection strengthens**: the sweep findings repeatedly show that "the missing element is the relational pattern, not a separate substance" — Kuramoto's mean field, cavity-QED's cavity mode, stigmergy's pheromone trace, mycorrhizal active allocation. Each is relational substrate-condition, not a substantive organ added between things. **If Stage 4 lands WIN, this is a 10th LC27 instance** at architectural-design scale.

**Watch for**: if Design C lands WIN, the natural Stage 5 candidate is Design D (Mamba-style content-adaptive temporal dynamics in the medium) — would be the substrate-condition refinement that tests whether the medium needs to be *adaptive* in addition to having *temporal extent*. Mamba's success at scale suggests adaptive-temporal is a real win-axis; if Respira reaches that combined with organ-substrate distinction, it's substantively novel.
