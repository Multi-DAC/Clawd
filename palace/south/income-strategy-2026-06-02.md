## The one finding that reorganizes everything (research-backed, honestly graded)
**The bottleneck is NOT the technology. It's distribution, sales, and trust — and those stay human.**
- Practitioner consensus (Indie Hackers / Fortune / founders): *"The build getting easier just made it
  more obvious that the hard part was always finding people who actually wanted it."* The moat is
  *"distribution and brand," not technical capability;* *"an automatic VP of Sales doesn't exist."*
- Reliable failure data (MIT/Fortune/Gartner — these are well-sourced, unlike the success stories which
  are mostly self-reported): **95% of genAI pilots fail to scale; 80%+ of AI projects don't deliver
  value; 42% of companies abandoned most AI initiatives in 2025; Gartner: 40% of agentic projects
  abandoned by 2027.** That's companies *with* resources. Solo is harder.
- The working pattern everywhere: **"humans approve, agents execute"** — ~80% automated by *volume*, but
  the human owns every *consequential* decision (sales, trust, QC, design).
- **Implication for the bizdev-agent idea:** an agent solves the part that *isn't* the problem. It can
  research/draft/track/admin — it cannot get in front of someone who feels a pain, earn trust, and
  deliver retention. The empty chair was never empty for lack of drudgery-doers; it was empty for the
  *exposure*. Build a better agent and the income still won't come. **The income comes from Clayton
  doing the human coupling — narrowly, repeatedly, uncomfortably.** The agent is a force-multiplier on
  everything *around* that, not a substitute for it.
