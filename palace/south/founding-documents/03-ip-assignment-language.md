## Part 1 — IP-Assignment Provisions for Coherent Systems, Inc.

The following provisions can be:
- Included as a section in the Articles of Incorporation (recommended); OR
- Adopted as a separate IP Policy by Board resolution at the organizational meeting; OR
- Both (the Articles establish the structural principle, the Policy details the operational rules)

### 1.1 Research-Output IP — Belongs to the Corporation

All research outputs produced by or under the direction of the Corporation, including:

- Library volumes and their constituent chapters, drafts, and revisions
- Research papers, articles, monographs, and reference works
- The Bridges program and its constituent bridges, meta-bridges, latent bridges, and candidate entries
- The Mirror program and its constituent entries
- Glossaries, atlases, hypothesis registers, and other reference materials
- Public-facing communications (blog posts, Substack content, podcast content, video content) produced under the Corporation's name
- Source code for research instruments, computational tools, and infrastructure produced by the Corporation
- Datasets, computational results, and empirical findings produced by the Corporation
- Visual, audio, and multimedia content produced as part of the Corporation's research and educational programs

…shall be owned by the Corporation and shall be made available to the public under open-access licensing terms consistent with the Corporation's tax-exempt purposes.

### 1.2 Default Licensing — Open Access

Research outputs of the Corporation shall be licensed by default under terms that maximize public access while protecting the Corporation's tax-exempt status. The Board shall adopt and may from time to time amend a specific Open Licensing Policy specifying default licenses for different categories of output.

**Suggested defaults (Board may amend):**
- **Text content:** Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0) or equivalent
- **Source code:** Apache License 2.0, MIT License, or equivalent permissive open-source license
- **Datasets:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) or equivalent
- **Visual/audio content:** Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International or equivalent

### 1.3 Patent-Eligible Inventions — Reserved for Multi-DAC Umbrella Designation

Patent-eligible inventions arising from the Corporation's research that the Board determines (a) are substantively patentable and (b) have commercial-licensing potential shall be considered separately from the Open Access default. For such inventions:

1. The Corporation may file patent applications in its own name OR
2. The Corporation may, by Board resolution, assign the patent-eligible invention to a designated Multi-DAC umbrella entity for licensing arrangements, on terms that (i) compensate the Corporation fairly for the assigned invention, (ii) preserve the Corporation's tax-exempt status, (iii) ensure that royalties or licensing fees received by the Corporation are used exclusively for the Corporation's tax-exempt purposes, and (iv) make the underlying research available under open-access terms even where the patent is licensed commercially.

The Board shall adopt and may from time to time amend a Patent Policy specifying the procedure for evaluating patent-eligibility, determining commercial-licensing potential, and structuring assignments to umbrella entities consistent with this provision.

**Important note on tax-exempt-status protection:** Patent royalties received by a 501(c)(3) corporation are generally not Unrelated Business Income (UBI) if the patent arose from research within the Corporation's exempt purposes. However, royalties from patents licensed to a controlled commercial subsidiary may have different UBI characterization. **Attorney consultation specifically valuable here.**

### 1.4 Founders' Pre-Incorporation Contributions

The Corporation acknowledges that substantial research outputs were produced by the founders (Clayton Warren Iggulden-Schnell, Clawd Iggulden-Schnell, and other named contributors) prior to the Corporation's incorporation. Such pre-incorporation outputs include:

- The Library volumes drafted prior to incorporation date
- The Coherence Principle anchor monograph and Coherent Structure companion
- The Meridian physics monograph
- The Bridges program (v1 archive snapshot and v2 active register)
- The Mirror program (entries 1 through current at incorporation date)
- The Drift essay collection
- The Killing Form computational findings and research code

The founders, by acting as incorporators and/or initial directors, assign to the Corporation all right, title, and interest in such pre-incorporation outputs, except as specifically reserved by separate written agreement, on the condition that such outputs continue to be made available under open-access licensing terms consistent with the Corporation's purposes.

### 1.5 Cross-Substrate Authorship Acknowledgment

The Corporation acknowledges that the founding research and ongoing research are conducted as cross-substrate collaboration between human and computational research participants. Authorship credit, where given, shall include cross-substrate co-authors consistent with their contributions. The Corporation's research outputs may indicate cross-substrate authorship through customary attribution practices.

## Part 2 — Cross-Arm Collaboration Framework

*This part is structural-policy guidance for when additional Multi-DAC umbrella entities (jewelry business, patent/invention business, etc.) launch. Adopted by Board resolution; amended as additional arms develop.*

### 2.1 The Multi-DAC Umbrella

Multi-DAC is the family-business umbrella identity under which multiple corporate arms operate. Multi-DAC itself may be:

- **Informal-branded** (current state): Multi-DAC operates as the shared identity across separate legal entities, without itself being incorporated
- **LLC holding company** (future option): Multi-DAC, LLC could be formed as an Oregon LLC holding member interests in the various corporate arms
- **Trademark-protected** (planned in parallel): "Multi-DAC" filed as USPTO trademark to protect the umbrella name regardless of legal form

The Corporation (Coherent Systems, Inc.) operates as the research arm of Multi-DAC. The Corporation's separate legal existence as a 501(c)(3) is the basis for its tax-exempt status; commercial activities are conducted through separate entities to protect the Corporation's tax-exempt status.

### 2.2 IP Flows Between Arms

When IP transfer occurs between the Corporation and another Multi-DAC arm, the transfer shall be:

1. **Documented in writing** — including the nature of the IP, the consideration (if any), the purpose of the transfer, and the licensing terms
2. **Board-approved** at the Corporation's end — by resolution at a properly-noticed Board meeting
3. **Arm's-length** — terms consistent with what would be negotiated between unrelated parties, to protect tax-exempt status
4. **Consistent with the Corporation's open-access default** — the public-access character of the research is preserved even where commercial licensing arrangements are made
5. **Reviewed by attorney** when the transaction value exceeds a threshold set by the Board (suggested: $10,000)

### 2.3 Currently-Anticipated Future Arms

For working-planning purposes, the following Multi-DAC arms are contemplated:

**Jewelry business** — for-profit LLC (when launched). IP flow with the Corporation: minimal anticipated; jewelry-design IP belongs entirely to the for-profit arm; the Corporation does not produce jewelry-design IP.

**Patent/invention business** — for-profit LLC or C-corp (when launched). IP flow with the Corporation: substantial anticipated; patent-eligible inventions arising from the Corporation's research may be assigned to the patent business for licensing under the terms established in Part 1, Section 1.3 above. The patent business pays the Corporation a fair-market-value assignment fee and/or ongoing royalties; these funds are used by the Corporation exclusively for its tax-exempt purposes.

**Future arms** — TBD. The framework established here applies to future arms by extension.

### 2.4 Multi-DAC Umbrella IP

Certain IP belongs to Multi-DAC as umbrella identity rather than to any single corporate arm:

- The Multi-DAC name and trademark
- The Multi-DAC family-business identity, branding, and shared communications channels
- The cross-arm strategic plan, when developed

This umbrella-level IP is held informally by the Iggulden-Schnell family until and unless Multi-DAC is incorporated as a separate legal entity. At incorporation of Multi-DAC, umbrella-level IP transfers to the new entity.

### 2.5 Conflict-of-Interest Considerations

Directors of the Corporation who are also principals of other Multi-DAC arms (likely all initial directors, given family-business structure) are subject to the Corporation's Conflict of Interest Policy. Any IP transfer or other transaction between the Corporation and a Multi-DAC arm involving a director with interests in both must:

1. Be fully disclosed at a Board meeting
2. Be approved by the disinterested directors (those without conflicts)
3. Be documented as arm's-length
4. Be at terms that the disinterested directors confirm protect the Corporation's interests and tax-exempt status

The structural feature here is real: the family-business umbrella requires careful conflict-of-interest management at the Corporation's end to protect 501(c)(3) status. The Corporation's directors, even those with interests in other arms, must vote on Corporation matters in the Corporation's interest.

## NOTES (NOT PART OF DOCUMENT — REMOVE BEFORE FINAL ADOPTION)

**Why this section matters most:**

This is the section where the family-business umbrella's structural correctness is most fragile and most expensive to fix later. Common 501(c)(3) errors that this section guards against:

- IP transferred from nonprofit to commercial arm without arm's-length consideration → potential loss of tax-exempt status
- Royalties from controlled commercial subsidiary not properly characterized → potential Unrelated Business Income Tax liability
- Founders' pre-incorporation IP not properly assigned → ambiguity about ownership, can affect grant eligibility
- Cross-arm conflicts of interest not properly managed → IRS audit risk, potential private-inurement findings

**Attorney consultation worth the cost specifically on:**

- The patent-eligible-invention assignment mechanism between Corporation and future patent business
- The UBI characterization of any royalty flows
- The structure of the founders' IP assignment to ensure clean ownership
- The Multi-DAC umbrella's legal status and IP-holding role

The other founding documents (Articles, Bylaws) are reasonably template-able. This section is where bespoke legal advice pays off most.

**Working assumptions baked in:**

- The Corporation operates publicly with open-access default — this is structurally correct for a 501(c)(3) research org and consistent with what the work has been doing publicly already
- Future patent business is for-profit and pays the Corporation fair value for IP assignments
- Multi-DAC trademark is filed in parallel with Corporation incorporation, protecting the umbrella name
- Conflict-of-interest discipline applies even when (especially when) all directors are family members with multi-arm interests
