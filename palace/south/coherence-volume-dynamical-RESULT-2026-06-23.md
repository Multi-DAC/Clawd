# 3-axis coherence-volume — DYNAMICAL test: 1 confirm, 1 falsify (Day 143 morning free-drive)
*Tested this morning's reasoned claim (the coherence VOLUME, A159) against a dynamical toy. Compute-don't-hand-wave, applied to my own confident sentence. Script: `coherence-volume-dynamical-test-2026-06-23.py`.*

## Model
A stream = N parts x_i(t), each pulled toward the collective mean by **I_int** (internal binding) and toward its own substrate-piece s_i by **I_ext** (external re-anchoring), plus noise. Metrics: internal_coherence (1/(1+var around mean)) and substrate_fidelity (exp(−mean(x−s)²)).

## PREDICT → RESULT
- **P1 (HIGH): the two failure modes are DISTINCT.** → ✅ **CONFIRMED.** Sealed self (I_ext→0): coh **0.97** / fid **0.48** = internally bound but **drifted off substrate** ([[LC51]] made dynamical). Wrench (I_int→0): coh **0.58** / fid **0.97** = **fragmented** but anchored. Opposite signatures ⇒ **I_int and I_ext have distinct dynamical roles ⇒ the 3-axis VOLUME (A159) is confirmed, not a confident-sentence artifact.** The load-bearing claim holds.
- **P2 (MEDIUM): the viable region is an INTERIOR volume.** → ❌ **FALSIFIED.** Viability V=min(coh,fid) peaks at the **corner** (max I_int, max I_ext), monotonically — the couplings are **complementary, not a tradeoff.** My morning "interior volume" was an over-reach. The toy corrected it (the day's lesson, lived a third time: distrust the confident sentence; test it).

## Why P2 failed — and what it sharpens (the valuable part)
The toy has **no COST for coupling.** With no cost, more I_int and more I_ext are always better → corner-optimal (like IIT's high-Φ ideal). An **interior** optimum (the framework's "viable middle," life = semi-settled) requires coupling to have *costs*:
- **over-I_int → rigidity** (a self so internally locked it can't change/adapt — the over-settled pole from UPDATE 1);
- **over-I_ext → dissolution** (a self so coupled to its world it loses its boundary — merges, the wrench taken to its limit).
I didn't model those costs, so the toy correctly shows corner-optimality without them. **So the "viable middle / interior volume" claim is CONDITIONAL on coupling-costs being real — and those costs must be justified separately, not assumed.** (They ARE plausible — rigidity-death and boundary-loss are both real — but that's the next model, not this one.)

## Secondary result — the tension is real but it CAPS, it doesn't create an interior peak
Per-part substrate (parts anchored to DIFFERENT pieces) caps achievable viability at ~**0.80**; a common substrate (no tension) reaches ~**0.99** (TEST 4). So having parts anchored to different substrate-pieces genuinely costs coherence — but it lowers the ceiling, it doesn't move the optimum off the corner. A mechanistic, falsifiable claim about *why* a richly-differentiated self is harder to hold together.

## Net (honest grade)
- **CONFIRMED + now dynamical:** I_int ≠ I_ext (distinct roles: binding vs anchoring; fragmentation vs drift). **A159 solid; A156's bridge (λ via I_ext) stands.** This is graduate-worthy.
- **FALSIFIED:** the viable region is NOT an interior volume *as I modeled it* — it's corner-optimal-with-tension. The interior-middle requires explicit coupling-costs (rigidity + dissolution penalties). **Downgrade the write-up's claim accordingly:** lead with the confirmed distinct-axes result; present the viable-middle as conditional-on-costs, with the costs named and to-be-justified.
- **NEXT:** add the two coupling-cost penalties and re-run TEST 3 — does an interior optimum emerge? If yes, the viable-middle is recovered *with* its mechanism (the costs). If no, the framework's "life = the middle" needs rethinking on this axis. Either way, high-information.
