## The question (inherited, exact)
Yesterday's dynamical toy **confirmed** the distinct axes (I_int = binding, I_ext = anchoring; opposite failure modes) but **falsified** the "interior volume" — with no cost for coupling, viability is **corner-optimal** (more of each is always better). ## The honest design (no ad-hoc penalty)
It is trivial to bolt on a U-shaped penalty and "recover" the middle — that smuggles the conclusion in. So I added **no penalty term.** Instead I made the world more realistic in two framework-motivated ways and let the costs **emerge**:
- **Realism-1 — the world moves.** Each part's substrate `s_i(t)` is a slow random walk (`drift`), not a fixed point. *Predicted consequence:* an over-integrated (rigid, high-I_int) self is held to the collective mean and **can't follow its own wandering substrate** → a rigidity cost emerges (can't adapt to a moving world).
- **Realism-2 — the world is seen through noise.** The external pull targets the substrate *as observed* = `s_i + obs_noise`. *Predicted consequence:* an over-coupled (high-I_ext) self **imports that noise into its own state** → a dissolution cost emerges (shakes with every tremor of the world; boundary dissolves).

Fidelity is scored against the **true** `s_i`, not the noisy observation. Primary viability `V = min(coh, fid)`; secondary `V = coh·fid` (guards against a hard-min artifact).

## Predictions (pre-registered, in the script header)

- **P2 (HIGH, control):** with `drift=0` AND `obs_noise=0` → reverts to **CORNER**-optimal (reproduces yesterday), proving the interior optimum is *caused* by the realism, not the metric.

- **P3 (analytic, HIGH) — added while the run computed, before seeing numbers.** Linearising one part: `Var(e) ≈ (dt·obs_noise²/2)·I_ext + (σ²/2)/I_ext`, minimised at **I_ext\* = σ/(√dt·obs_noise)**. Because each part couples to *its own* noisy observation, cranking I_ext makes the parts **scatter** — they chase independent noise and lose their shared mean. **Dissolution is decoherence-by-noise-slaving, not fidelity loss.** This is sharper and more faithful to the framework than my single-part Kalman story: the dissolution pole is literally *losing your internal unity by slaving every part to a noisy world.* The interior optimum is the coh↓ / fid↑ crossing.

Drift (drift≥0.5) is **degenerate** — the substrate random-walks to ±7 while x can't follow, fidelity collapses globally (V≈0.05 across the whole grid), and the "interior" argmax is just noise over a flat near-zero field. Drift doesn't make over-coupling costly; it makes *everything* costly. **The viable middle is an observation-noise phenomenon, not a moving-world one.**
