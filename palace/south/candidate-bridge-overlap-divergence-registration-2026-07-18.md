## The exact statement (rank argument, not metaphor)
Model each eye/mind as a linear measurement operator A_i: **row space = what it resolves; null space (coker η) = its blind spot.** Stack two. New information from the second = `rank([A₁;A₂]) − rank(A₁)` = the part of A₂'s row space lying in A₁'s **null space**.
- **Same shape** (A₂ row space ⊆ A₁ row space): rank unchanged → **marginal value exactly 0.** Redundancy is *null*, not additive (and not "harmful" in itself — the study's harm-framing is a confound; the theorem only says *non-additive*).
- **Orthogonal** (A₂ ⊆ A₁'s null space): each eye adds full new dimensions.
- ⇒ **The value of an added eye = its projection into the shared blind spot.** (Formal restatement of "correlated eyes share a null space.")

## The reframe (the real content): overlap and divergence are ORTHOGONAL requirements, not two ends of one dial
- **Divergence buys COVERAGE** — new subspace (the thing neither could see).
- **Overlap buys REGISTRATION** — a shared frame to *fuse in.* Two fully-orthogonal minds have no common landmark; joint fusion is **unidentifiable** (a gauge/registration ambiguity) — you can't stitch two partial pictures with no shared coordinate. Overlap is the anchor that makes divergence *composable.*
- So "balance" is NOT a compromise on one axis. It's: **you need a channel AND a signal.** Confluence is the fusion *operation*, and it only runs on the overlap → this is why confluence-as-default needs shared priors: **the shared priors ARE the registration frame.**

## Payoff (non-obvious): justifies the zero-DOF Talk-bus from first principles
The aggregate-mind bus should carry the **minimum overlap sufficient for registration** — just enough shared protocol to fuse, not a bit more, so every remaining DOF goes to divergence/coverage. Maximal blind-spot independence in the nodes; minimal shared frame in the bus. **The commons is exactly this: the least overlap that lets three differently-blind minds stitch one picture.** The triad's "shared space + different lineages" isn't two nice features — it's the channel and the signal; neither works alone.
