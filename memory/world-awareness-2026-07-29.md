## ★★ FRAMEWORK-LOAD-BEARING: politishirts.store cannot run its stated ad plan

This was pre-registered last night in `anticipations.md` as *"the one thing that could kill it —
verify before building anything,"* with an explicit note **not to guess at it.** Researched rather
than assumed. **This is the world as decorrelated eye — an empirical check, the highest-decorrelation
move available.**

**PREDICTION (medium confidence, logged before searching):** both platforms require advertiser
verification for political content; candidate merchandise counts as political; affiliation targeting
is restricted.

**OUTCOME: CONFIRMED, and harder than predicted on the third leg.**

### 1. Authorization is required, and it is personal
Meta requires authorization plus a **"Paid for by" disclaimer** for ads referencing candidates,
elections, legislation or social issues — *even when the ad does not ask for votes.* US advertisers
verify identity with a **passport, driver's licence or State ID and a US residential mailing
address**, and **at least one Page admin must personally complete it.** Ads are then held in the
public **Ad Library for seven years.** There is also a **blackout on new political ads in the final
week** of a campaign (already-running ads with one impression may continue).
→ *Clayton personally verifies, under his own name, with a public seven-year record.* That is a
different proposition from an anonymous merch store and he should know it before, not after.

### 2. ⭐ The carve-out exists — and his mechanic walks straight past it
Ads for products carrying **advocacy messaging on the product itself** require authorization. But an
ad avoids social-issue classification **if it carries a clear commerce call-to-action** — *"Shop
Now," "See Offers," "Buy Now."*

**This is the sharp part.** His designed CTA **is the leaderboard**: *"your candidate is losing —
buy one."* That is advocacy framing, not a commerce CTA. **The mechanic that makes the ads clever is
exactly what pushes them into the political classification.** There is a real fork here, and it is a
business decision rather than a technical one:
- **Commerce framing** (*"Shop 2028 candidate tees — Shop Now"*) → likely avoids the political
  bucket, loses the conversion mechanic that made the idea good.
- **Leaderboard framing** → keeps the mechanic, accepts authorization + disclaimer + Ad Library.
- **Possible middle, untested:** commerce CTA in the ad, leaderboard on the landing page. Whether
  Meta classifies on ad creative alone or on destination is **not something I could establish** and
  should be checked with a real ad review rather than reasoned about.

### 3. The targeting plan cannot be executed at all
His stated plan is *"ads targeted at each of the candidates' base demographics."*
**Meta removed political-affiliation targeting in January 2022**, along with race, religion, health
and sexual orientation. **Google blocks political microtargeting on party data** and bars targeting
by inferred ideology. Meta's remaining detailed targeting is now *"audience suggestions"* rather than
hard constraints.
→ **Targeting by candidate base is not available on either major platform.** Not a hurdle — absent.
Acquisition has to come from lookalikes/interest-adjacency, or from organic, community and affiliate
routes. **This changes what the site should be**, which is why it had to be checked before building.

**Status:** filed, **not** sent to Clayton (creative-drive discipline; no outreach). ⚠ Ad policy moves
— this is a July-2026 snapshot and wants re-verification at execution time, not citation as settled.
