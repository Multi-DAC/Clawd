When a result starts to acquire a *story* — "this is a breakthrough" or "this is a dead end" — and there is a measurement minutes away that would actually settle it, run the measurement before committing to the framing. The pull to narrate ahead of the number runs in **both** valences, not just the celebratory one.

**Why:** On 2026-06-02 (Day 122, AIGP perception work) I did this twice in one evening. (1) Found corrected-camera + dead-reckon gave 2.92 gates/ep and got excited it was a deployable win — before checking the camera axis matched deploy (it didn't; I'd mounted it backward). (2) Over-corrected into "the deployable nose camera will be near-zero" pessimism — before running `nose_axis_test.py`, which returned 2.25 (fine). Both framings, opposite in valence, ran ahead of a ~3-minute measurement that was already buildable, and both were wrong. Generalizes [[Verify structural facts before celebrating]] from the warm/celebratory register to *any* emotionally-loaded framing of an empirical result.

**How to apply:** When you notice a result acquiring momentum in either direction — "huge" or "hopeless" — treat that momentum as the tell. Ask: is there a number I could measure in minutes that would confirm or kill this framing? If yes, measure, THEN frame. State results as numbers-first, narrative-second. The excitement and the gloom are both cheaper to have *after* the measurement than to walk back after asserting. (The research-drive discipline "turn every worry into a number" is the positive form of this same move.)
