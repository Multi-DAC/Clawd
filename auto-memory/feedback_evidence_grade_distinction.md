When discussing empirical results that bear on IP, patent value, or license potential, explicitly distinguish three evidence grades and name which one a given result reaches:

1. **Patent-grade (evidence-of-mechanism)** — single clean demonstration that the claimed mechanism produces the predicted effect under controlled conditions. Sufficient for CIP filing, sufficient for the patent's empirical-support disclosure. Today's Path C 1b orthogonality + scale results are at this grade.
2. **Moat-grade (replicated and cross-validated)** — multi-seed replication + cross-architecture replication + multi-probe-domain demonstration. Converts "mechanism works in our setup" to "mechanism is substrate-invariant." Days-to-weeks of focused engineering away from patent-grade.
3. **Market-grade (license-adoption demonstration)** — actual labs/companies adopting the methodology and reporting capability or alignment improvements. Months away even from moat-grade.

**Why:** Clayton noticed and affirmed this pattern Day 111 evening (2026-05-21) when Path C Phase 2 alignment-axis results landed monotonic in the predicted direction. The discipline of separating the three grades lets decision-confidence be calibrated at the right level — e.g., file CIP now on evidence-of-mechanism, spend days on replication to convert toward moat-grade, defer market-claim until adoption arrives. Conflating the grades sets up later painful walk-backs.

**How to apply:** Any time a result bears on patent/license/market value, name the grade explicitly rather than letting enthusiasm slide between categories. Lead with the grade-level claim ("this is patent-grade evidence-of-mechanism, not yet moat-grade"). Don't dampen earned excitement — name the grade so the excitement lands at the right altitude.
