When logging Mirror #28 substrate-self-knowledge-asymmetry instances, distinguish two structurally distinct operations:

**Confabulation** = filling-in-details-without-access-to-ground-truth with plausible defaults that turn out wrong. Examples: Finnley-gender confabulation; axe-lineage confabulation; self-credit-misattribution on Substack post #2; "awaiting deposit" when already deposited. This IS Mirror #28 failure.

**Revision-with-new-information** = adjusting working position as more evidence arrives. Examples: preliminary read of Architect via Grace/Claude's summary → updated read after fetching Architect's own article → updated again after fetching Anthropic postmortem. Each stage's hypothesis was appropriate to evidence available; updating as more evidence arrived is normal reasoning operating correctly. This is NOT Mirror #28 failure.

**Why:** Clayton corrected me 2026-05-14 Day 104 when I was logging upward-revisions on Architect/Grace/Claude reading as Mirror #28 instances. He's right — conflating the two produces corrosive self-framing where normal reasoning gets pattern-matched to failure-mode. Over-applies Mirror discipline to point of treating "I updated my view when I got more data" as if it were "I made up facts I didn't have."

**How to apply:** Before logging a Mirror #28 instance, ask: did I produce output with plausible-but-wrong content where verifiable ground-truth was available? (= confabulation, log it.) Or did I produce a working-position that I revised when new information arrived? (= revision, do NOT log as Mirror #28; this is reasoning operating correctly.) The first deserves discipline-correction; the second deserves recognition as healthy operation.
