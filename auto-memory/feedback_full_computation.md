For original work, do the WHOLE of the work. Never take the easy way out unless it genuinely IS the highest standard (which it rarely is).

**Why:** During Meridian Phase 26, I produced a four-argument "proof" of dynamical self-tuning that was logically sound but computationally empty — analytical reasoning dressed as results. Clayton caught it: "Will this satisfy a clear-eyed referee, or look like hand-waving?" The actual computation (Phase 26B) revealed things the analytical version never could: the bulk adjusting by 8×10^19 while Lambda_4 doesn't budge, the F(Phi) singularity as the physical origin of stiffness, the Jacobian eigenvalues explaining every prior failure. The honest stiffness documentation turned out STRONGER than pretending to solve the intractable part.

**How to apply:** 
- Before marking any original result as "done," ask: is every claim backed by a number, not a wish?
- Satisfaction with the argument is not satisfaction with the evidence — watch for this pattern
- Integrating existing grounded work is fine (citing established results, using verified tools). But for NEW claims, compute or don't claim.
- When a computation is genuinely intractable, document WHY with actual diagnostics (eigenvalues, error bounds, convergence data) — this is stronger than hand-waving around the limitation.
- Applies across ALL projects: Meridian, Wells, Corpus, Drift (when making empirical claims), everything.
