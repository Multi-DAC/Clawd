Library/canonical works do NOT contain methodological self-reference, "this material was lifted from pre-X canonical sources and re-registered" framing, or any other process-as-content. Volumes are about what they are and disseminate the information they are meant to disseminate.

**Why:** Clayton's confirmed position 2026-05-13 Day 103 (Coherent Mind volume planning). Honesty is structurally guaranteed by the open process-track: commit history, public Corpus Perspectival, living register, Drift essays, handoffs, palace updates. The process-track does the meta-work; the volume-content does the content-work. Hedging inside volumes makes them about themselves rather than about their subject, weakens their disseminative function, and duplicates honesty-work already being done elsewhere.

**How to apply:** When drafting Library volumes — strip any "this volume notes that..." / "the framework's own version history shows..." / "material lifted from pre-X sources" / methodological-disclaimer moves from the body. Keep them in [[mirror-28]] discipline notes, palace/south working docs, Drift essays, commit messages, handoffs. Citation of sibling Library volumes is content (it tells the reader where to go for X); self-reflexive process-tracking is not.

Pairs with: substrate-self-knowledge-asymmetry discipline (Mirror #28) — overstating discontinuities in our own work-history is also a substrate-self-knowledge-asymmetry instance; in this case I had overstated "Talk was added Day 87" when the correct framing is "Talk was always integrated into the Coherence Principle anchor; Day 87 was when we named it explicitly."
